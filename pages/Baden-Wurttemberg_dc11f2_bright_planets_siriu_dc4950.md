## Introduction

Bright planets and stars are one of the most important mundane triggers behind UFO reports in Baden-Württemberg. The key point is not that witnesses are foolish, but that Venus, Jupiter and Sirius can be genuinely striking when they appear low over a town, above a ridge, beside the Moon, or in morning twilight when people do not expect to see “stars” still shining. CENAP, the civilian reporting centre with roots in Mannheim, has repeatedly linked recent German UFO-reporting waves to exactly these objects. In 2024, Baden-Württemberg produced 153 UFO reports to CENAP, with regional clusters around [Lake Constance]({{'lake-constance/' | relative_url }}), the Markgräflerland and the [Rhine-Neckar]({{'rhine-neckar/' | relative_url }}) area; CENAP attributed many reports that year to bright planets such as Venus and Jupiter and bright stars such as Sirius, not to alien craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.swr.de/swraktuell/baden-wuerttemberg/rekord-ufo-meldungen-100.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: swr.de">[SWR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">swr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ein UFO über BW? ## Why bright planets become UFO reports

Venus is the classic UFO trigger because it is both ordinary and oddly theatrical. It does not cross the sky like an aircraft, it does not trail like a meteor, and it can appear to hover over the same patch of landscape for long enough to feel deliberate. The Royal Observatory Greenwich notes that Venus is best seen as an evening or morning object and that, when near the horizon, atmospheric twinkling can produce flashing colour effects that are reported as strange objects or UFOs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/space-astronomy/planet-venus" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rmg.co.uk">[Royal Museums Greenwich]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rmg.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Royal Museums GreenwichPlanet VenusUnlike the Earth, Venus has a very small magnetic field apart from that induced by the effect of the s...</span></span></span> NASA’s Night Sky Network gives the basic geometry behind this: because Venus orbits inside Earth’s orbit, it is seen before sunrise or after sunset, rather than high in the middle of the night.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nightsky.jpl.nasa.gov/news/190/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nightsky.jpl.nasa.gov">[nightsky.jpl.nasa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nightsky.jpl.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Spot the Morning and Evening Star: Observe Venus1 Mar 2023 — Earth-based observers can observe Venus in the morning before sunrise, or in...</span></span></span>

That timing is crucial for Baden-Württemberg reports. A person looking out from a balcony in Stuttgart, walking a dog near Freiburg, driving along the Upper Rhine, or standing by Lake Constance at dusk may see a brilliant fixed point where they expected aircraft lights, not a planet. If the witness has no familiar reference point, the brain may turn brightness into proximity: a planet looks “low”, so it feels nearby; it is bright, so it feels large; it does not move like a plane, so it feels controlled.

Jupiter works slightly differently. It is usually less dazzling than Venus, but it can still dominate a clear sky and become conspicuous when paired with Venus or the Moon. In August 2025, CENAP received many reports after Jupiter and Venus appeared close together in the morning sky. German public broadcaster ZDF reported that callers described the sight as “drone twins”, “heavenly car headlights” or stars that should not exist; the explanation was the two bright planets standing close together from Earth’s perspective. ZDF also noted that most enquiries came from Hessen, Rhineland-Palatinate and Baden-Württemberg.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.zdfheute.de/panorama/lichter-himmel-ufo-jupiter-venus-100.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zdfheute.de">[ZDFheute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zdfheute.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ungewöhnliche Lichter am Himmel sind keine Ufos</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ungewöhnliche Lichter am Himmel sind keine Ufos</span></span></span>

The most useful lesson is that a sighting can feel unusual precisely because it is real. Venus and Jupiter are not vague smudges. They are bright, naked-eye objects. When they appear together, line up with the Moon, or hang above a familiar roofline, they can become a memorable sky event for people who do not regularly follow astronomy calendars.<img src="{{"/assets/images/Baden-Wurttemberg_dc11f2_bright_planets_siriu_dc4950-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Bright Lights illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Baden-Wurttemberg_dc11f2_bright_planets_siriu_dc4950-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Baden-Wurttemberg_dc11f2_bright_planets_siriu_dc4950-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## How Venus and Jupiter fool the eye

The main visual trap is “hovering”. Aircraft lights usually change position, sound may follow, and navigation lights blink in recognisable patterns. A planet, by contrast, appears fixed over minutes. It does move across the sky as Earth rotates, but that movement is slow enough that a casual observer may read it as stationary. The Royal Observatory Greenwich’s guide to bright objects notes that Venus and Jupiter are likely explanations for very bright white points of light and that planets keep their position relative to surrounding stars as the sky turns.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/space-astronomy/what-was-bright-object-i-saw-sky-last-night" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rmg.co.uk">[Royal Museums Greenwich]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rmg.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on rmg.co.uk.</span></span></span>

A second trap is twilight contrast. Venus is bright enough to remain visible when other stars have faded, which makes it look “out of place”. During the August 2025 Venus-Jupiter reports, CENAP explained through German media that the two planets were especially bright and still visible in morning twilight, while ordinary stars were no longer obvious.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.anews.com.tr/world/2025/08/12/ufo-reporting-up-in-germany-as-people-misidentify-planets" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: anews.com.tr">[A News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">anews.com.tr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">A News UFO reporting up in Germany as people misidentify planets</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A News UFO reporting up in Germany as people misidentify planets</span></span></span> That is exactly the sort of condition that can produce a credible but mistaken report: the witness sees something real, bright and isolated, then compares it with a sky that otherwise appears empty.

A third trap is landscape anchoring. In Baden-Württemberg, many reports are likely to be framed against hills, church towers, forest edges, airport approaches, industrial lights, or the Rhine-Neckar urban skyline. A bright planet just above the horizon may seem to sit over a village or military site, even though it is astronomical. This is why sighting location and direction matter so much. A report from the Lake Constance area, for example, cannot be assessed simply by saying “a light was seen over the lake”; investigators need the time, compass direction and elevation above the horizon.

## Why Sirius can look strange near the horizon

Sirius is not a planet, but it belongs in this discussion because it creates a different kind of UFO report: the flashing, colour-changing “object”. It is the brightest star visible from Earth, and when it sits low in the sky its light passes through more of the atmosphere. EarthSky explains that Sirius can flash different colours near the horizon because atmospheric refraction breaks starlight into its component colours; the effect is more noticeable because Sirius is so bright.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://earthsky.org/tonight/the-skys-brightest-star-sirius-before-dawn/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: earthsky.org">[EarthSky]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">earthsky.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Earth Sky See brightest star, Sirius, in your morning sky</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Earth Sky See brightest star, Sirius, in your morning sky</span></span></span>

This makes Sirius a natural trigger for reports that sound more dramatic than a simple “bright light”. Witnesses may describe red, blue, green or white flashes, apparent pulsing, or an object that seems to tremble in place. Those descriptions can sound mechanical or technological, especially if the observer is already thinking of drones. Yet they are often a product of atmospheric turbulence, low altitude and human perception rather than a moving craft.

The distinction between planets and Sirius is useful in investigation. Planets generally twinkle less than stars because their apparent discs average out some atmospheric turbulence; the Royal Observatory Greenwich notes this difference in its bright-object guide.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/space-astronomy/what-was-bright-object-i-saw-sky-last-night" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rmg.co.uk">[Royal Museums Greenwich]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rmg.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on rmg.co.uk.</span></span></span> Sirius, as a point-like star, is more vulnerable to scintillation, the technical term for twinkling. So a steady, very bright white object near dusk may point towards Venus or Jupiter, while a colour-flashing object low in the winter sky may point towards Sirius or another bright star.

For Baden-Württemberg, Sirius is especially relevant because many reports come from ordinary settings rather than organised skywatching. A witness may not know which part of the sky Sirius occupies in a given season, but they will notice a bright light that appears to blink over rooftops, hills or woodland. ## What details separate astronomy from aircraft

The simplest way to test a bright-light report is not to ask whether the witness “believes in UFOs”. It is to reconstruct the sky. CENAP’s own public advice, as reported by SWR, is that a useful report should include the date, time, observation place and direction; with those details, the centre can check air traffic, satellites, rocket bodies and astronomical causes such as bright planets or fixed stars.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ardsounds.de/episode/urn%3Aard%3Asection%3A87bb59a2d2129759/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ardsounds.de">[ARD Sounds]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ardsounds.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ARD Sounds UFO-Meldestelle CENAP: &quot;In den meisten Fällen sind es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ARD Sounds UFO-Meldestelle CENAP: &quot;In den meisten Fällen sind es</span></span></span>

For a public-facing Baden-Württemberg UFO page, the practical distinction is this:

**Likely Venus or Jupiter:** a very bright white or slightly yellow point; visible after sunset or before sunrise; apparently stationary over several minutes; no engine noise; no rapid change in direction; often near the western or eastern horizon depending on the season.

**Likely Sirius or another bright star:** a bright point lower in the sky; strong twinkling; apparent red, blue, green or white flashes; no steady travel path; often more dramatic when viewed through turbulent air, thin cloud, window glass or from a heated urban environment.

**More likely aircraft or drone:** clear movement relative to buildings or stars; blinking navigation lights; changing brightness as the object turns; sound, if nearby; a path consistent with an airport corridor, emergency helicopter route, or local drone use.

## Why this pattern keeps returning

The pattern keeps returning because astronomy, attention and media timing overlap. Bright planets have cycles. Conjunctions occur. Sirius rises and sets with the seasons. At the same time, more people carry phones, share images quickly and are primed by news about drones, satellites, military objects and unidentified aerial phenomena.
